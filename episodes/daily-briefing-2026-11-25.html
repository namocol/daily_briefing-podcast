And now for the Microsoft three sixty-five and Entra news. There are two stories you should know about, and the first one is a must-act item.

Microsoft has disclosed a data exposure in Entra Connect Health. A flaw in the Connect Health A.P.I., introduced on July twenty-third, let unauthorized parties read tenant-level metadata. That included administrator and technical contact email addresses, tenant IDs, domain names, and sync configuration details. Microsoft says the access was read-only. It found the flaw on November ninth and deployed a server-side fix on November nineteenth. Reports say attackers did exploit it.

Here's why it matters to you. Any client tenant running Entra Connect Health may have had admin contact emails exposed. Those emails are perfect targets for phishing. Check the Message Center and Service Health for each tenant you manage to see whether Microsoft notified you, and warn your admins to expect targeted phishing. Expect lures that mention Microsoft security notices.

The second story is the root cause of the November twelfth outage. Microsoft's preliminary post-incident report says the nearly twenty-four-hour outage that took down Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, and Copilot came from three factors lining up. First, there was a latent lock-contention bug in the networking gateway servers, also introduced in July. Second, Teams traffic failed over onto the Exchange gateways. Third, about fourteen percent of gateway capacity was offline for maintenance. Microsoft says it was not a cyberattack, and no changes are needed on customer Windows machines. If any of your clients asked what happened, this is your answer.

Also on the calendar this month, Microsoft is retiring SharePoint one-time passcode authentication for external sharing by the end of November. If any of your clients' external guests still sign in with a passcode, move them to Entra B2B guest accounts now.

Now for threats and vulnerabilities.

The headline is a breach at U.S. intelligence agencies. The C.I.A. and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence confirmed that an unclassified open-source intelligence system was breached. Officials say no classified information was affected, and a source told the Associated Press the issue has been resolved. The New York Times reported that at least three agencies were affected and that the attackers appear to have used an A.I.-powered tool.

On the known exploited vulnerabilities list, CISA added two entries on Monday. Federal agencies must patch both by tomorrow, November twenty-sixth. The first is relevant to the MSP world: a server-side request forgery flaw in N-able Mail Assure, formerly SpamExperts, rated nine point three. If you or any clients use Mail Assure, confirm you're on the patched build. The second is a critical path traversal flaw in MONAI Label, a medical imaging A.I. tool.
