Now let's talk shop. Microsoft published its final report on the November twelfth outage that took down Exchange Online for the better part of two days. The root cause was a Windows defect in the configuration of Exchange's gateway servers, introduced by a code change back in July. At its worst, more than eighty percent of North American gateway servers stopped responding. Here's the uncomfortable part: Microsoft's load testing ran on older hardware with fewer cores, so it missed a bug that got worse on newer machines. Microsoft says no queued enterprise mail was lost, after it stretched the retry window to forty-eight hours. Internally, Microsoft declared a "Code Red," with M365 Core President Jason Henderson calling it the most customer-impacting incident in Exchange Online's history. The company says it's aiming to resolve future worldwide incidents within one hour.

Why it matters to you: save the incident details from Service Health for your tenants now, because it only keeps thirty days of history. And if a client took a real hit, look into whether they qualify for an S.L.A. credit. Check the claim rules in Microsoft's own S.L.A. terms.

Two deadlines are worth your calendar too. According to Microsoft's retirement schedule, basic authentication for S.M.T.P. AUTH in Exchange Online gets disabled by default at the end of December, with full retirement for client submission in January. That's every old scanner, copier, and line-of-business app that sends mail with a username and password. Inventory them now and move them to O.Auth or a relay. Then on February first, Entra retires built-in S.M.S. and voice M.F.A. Start moving those users to Authenticator or passkeys over the holidays, while things are quiet.

Here's the one you'll want to act on today. Drupal sites are under active attack, from two different flaws. The first is a remote code execution bug in the Drupal installer. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and the federal due date is today, November twenty-seventh. The second, disclosed Wednesday, is a core access bypass that lets an attacker take over a site without logging in. It hits sites left in an incomplete install state, and Drupal has confirmed attacks in the wild. The fix is Drupal ten point six point twenty-three, eleven point three point twenty-three, or eleven point four point fourteen. Run update dot P.H.P. after you upgrade, block the install script, and review activity on the user one admin account. If the church site or any client site runs Drupal, that's your first move.

Also on the KEV list: a VMware vCenter authentication bypass that a suspected China-linked group has exploited since early September. Broadcom patched it on November nineteenth. Two other items landed in that same batch, for the Optimizely platform and the Idunno AntiCsrf library, also due today.
